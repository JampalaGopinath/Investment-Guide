# Chapter 15 — ₹2,00,000 Real Comparison

!!! note "Side heading: Your exact scenario"
    Two investments of ₹1,00,000 each — one for each grandchild.  
    This chapter shows what each allocation strategy looks like over 5 and 10 years.

## Option 1 — Both into NSC

| Milestone | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Year 5 | ₹2,89,800 |
| Year 10 | ₹4,20,000 (assuming similar rates on reinvestment) |

**Pros:**
- Maximum corpus growth
- Zero maintenance or operational effort

**Cons:**
- Completely illiquid for the full 5 years
- No access in an emergency before maturity

!!! note "Side heading: Best if…"
    Both grandchildren are young and there is **no foreseeable need** to touch the money before Year 5.

## Option 2 — Both into SBI FD

| Milestone | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Year 5 | ₹2,83,600 |
| Year 10 | ₹3,95,200 (assuming renewal at similar rate) |

**Pros:**
- Full flexibility — can break if needed
- Manageable via YONO app

**Cons:**
- Slightly lower return than NSC
- Premature withdrawal penalty reduces effective return if broken early

!!! note "Side heading: Best if…"
    You want to keep both options open and are uncertain about future needs.

## Option 3 — Mixed: ₹1L NSC + ₹1L SBI FD ⭐ Recommended

| Milestone | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Year 5 | ₹2,86,700 |
| Year 10 | ₹4,07,600 |

**Pros:**
- Best balance of growth and liquidity
- One leg is locked for maximum growth (NSC)
- One leg is flexible for emergencies (SBI FD)
- Different products reduce operational dependency on one institution

**Cons:**
- Slightly below Option 1 in total return

!!! note "Side heading: Why mixed is recommended"
    Life is unpredictable. A mixed allocation ensures you never have **all funds locked** at once while still getting NSC's superior compounding on half the corpus.
