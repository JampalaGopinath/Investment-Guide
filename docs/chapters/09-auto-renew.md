# Chapter 9 — Auto-Renew vs Manual Strategy

!!! note "Side heading: Default setting"
    Keep **auto-renew ON** as the default. But set a calendar reminder **1 week before maturity** to review before the renewal happens.

## Why auto-renew is helpful

- Prevents funds from sitting idle in a savings account at ~4% if you forget to act at maturity.
- Low-maintenance approach — suits the "set and forget" goal.
- Most SBI FDs and Post Office TDs support auto-renew.

!!! note "Side heading: Risk of auto-renew without review"
    If rates have fallen by the time your FD matures, auto-renew will lock you in at the new (lower) rate for another full term. A 1-week pre-maturity review prevents this.

## Best practice checklist

- [x] Enable auto-renew when opening the FD/TD
- [x] Note the exact maturity date in your phone or diary
- [x] Set a **calendar reminder 7 days before maturity**
- [ ] On that day: check current rates at SBI / Post Office
- [ ] Decide: allow renewal at new rate, or manually redirect funds

## Decision rule — 1 week before maturity

| Prevailing rate at maturity | Action |
|-----------------------------|--------|
| **High (≥ 7.0%)** | Allow auto-renewal — rate is still good |
| **Medium (6.5–6.9%)** | Review: consider shorter tenure or NSC instead |
| **Low (< 6.5%)** | Cancel renewal; redirect to better option |

## NSC note

NSC cannot be auto-renewed — it is a one-time certificate. At maturity, collect the payout and reinvest manually (check rates at that time before reinvesting).

!!! note "Side heading: NSC maturity action"
    Mark your NSC maturity date carefully. Post offices do not always notify you proactively.
