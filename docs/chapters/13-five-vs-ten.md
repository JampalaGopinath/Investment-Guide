# Chapter 13 — 5-Year vs 10-Year Strategy

!!! note "Side heading: Practical approach"
    Treat **5 years as a checkpoint**, not a final destination.  
    Do not assume today's rates will repeat in Years 6–10 — they may be higher or lower.

## Option A — 5-year lock (recommended)

- Invests for 5 years at current known rate
- Reviews rate environment at maturity (Year 5)
- Can choose best product in Year 6 based on prevailing rates
- Flexibility if grandchild needs funds sooner than expected

!!! note "Side heading: Why 5 years is safer"
    Rates change every quarter (NSC, PO TD are revised by the government). Locking for only 5 years gives you a clean decision point in Year 6 instead of being committed to unknown rates.

## Option B — 10-year commitment

- NSC itself is a **5-year product** — a "10-year NSC" does not exist
- "10 years" means: buy NSC → mature at Year 5 → buy again at Year 5 rate
- That second-cycle rate is completely unknown today
- Less flexibility if the grandchild needs money between Year 5 and Year 10

## Side-by-side

| Factor | 5-Year Plan | 10-Year Plan |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| Known rate | 5 years | 5 years only |
| Rate at Year 6 | Chosen at maturity | Unknown |
| Flexibility | Full after Year 5 | Partially locked again |
| Emergency access | Review at Year 5 | Locked again if reinvested |
| Suitable for | Most grandchildren scenarios | Only if child is very young (≤5 years) |

## Recommended approach

1. **Lock 5 years now** (NSC + SBI FD as recommended in [Chapter 16](16-recommended-plan.md))
2. **Set maturity reminder** (1 week before)
3. At Year 5: reassess — continue, redirect, or partially withdraw based on grandchild's needs and prevailing rates

!!! note "Side heading: When a 10-year view makes sense"
    If your grandchild is only 2–3 years old today and you definitely want the money untouched until they are 13–15, a two-cycle 5+5 approach is fine. Just budget for the Year 5 reinvestment step.
