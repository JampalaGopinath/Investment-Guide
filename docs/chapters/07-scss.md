# Chapter 7 — SCSS (Senior Citizen Saving Scheme)

!!! note "Side heading: Who it fits"
    SCSS is excellent for **quarterly income** — it is not designed for building the maximum corpus for children or grandchildren.

## Product details

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Type** | Senior Citizen Saving Scheme |
| **Eligibility** | Age 60+ (or 55+ on retirement) |
| **Lock-in** | 5 years (partial withdrawal after 1 year) |
| **Interest** | Paid out quarterly |
| **Rate** | 8.2% p.a. (March 2026) |
| **Maximum investment** | ₹30,00,000 per person |

## How interest works

!!! note "Side heading: Quarterly payout"
    Interest is paid four times a year directly to your savings account.  
    Example: ₹1,00,000 × (8.2% ÷ 4) = **₹2,050 every 3 months**.

Because interest leaves the scheme every quarter, SCSS has **no automatic compounding**. The headline rate of 8.2% is attractive but the effective compounded return is lower unless you reinvest every quarterly payout.

## Advantages

- Highest nominal rate among the four options (8.2%)
- Predictable quarterly cash flow — useful if grandparents need regular income
- Government-backed
- Partial withdrawal available after 1 year

## Major drawbacks for a "grandchildren corpus" goal

!!! note "Side heading: Drawbacks"
    SCSS is a regular-income product, not a wealth-accumulation product.  
    For building a corpus, compounding schemes (NSC, SBI FD) outperform it in practice.

- **No automatic compounding** — interest leaves the scheme every quarter
- Interest is fully taxable as income in the year received
- Requires quarterly reinvestment discipline to approach headline-rate returns
- Not designed for "corpus maximisation"

## Comparison — ₹1,00,000 for 5 years

| Metric | SCSS (8.2% payout) | NSC (7.7% compounding) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Quarterly income | ₹2,050 | None |
| Final amount (no reinvest) | ₹1,41,000 | ₹1,44,900 |
| Reinvestment burden | Very high (quarterly) | None |
| Tax on interest | Taxable | Taxable (accrue annually) |

!!! note "Side heading: The surprise"
    Despite SCSS paying 8.2% vs NSC's 7.7%, **NSC gives a higher final corpus** because it compounds while SCSS pays out. The extra 0.5% rate is consumed by the loss of compounding.
