# Chapter 8 — Interest Rate Strategy

!!! note "Side heading: Rule of thumb"
    **High rates → lock longer. Low rates → keep shorter.**  
    This simple rule helps you maximise return across changing rate environments.

## Rate-to-tenure decision table

| Rate Level | Condition | Recommended Action |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **High (≥ 7.5%)** | Favourable | Lock 4–5 years |
| **Medium (7.0–7.4%)** | Moderate | Lock 2–3 years |
| **Low (≤ 6.9%)** | Unfavourable | Prefer 6–12 months |

## March 2026 snapshot

!!! note "Side heading: Current positioning"
    Rates in March 2026 are at the high-to-medium range. This favours locking into longer tenures now.

| Scheme | Current Rate | Assessment | Recommended tenure |

| NSC (5 years) | 7.7% | High | 5 years ✓ |
| Post Office TD (5 years) | 7.5% | High | 5 years ✓ (if reinvesting interest) |
| SCSS | 8.2% | High | 5 years ✓ (if income-focused) |

## Why this matters for a 5–10 year plan

- Locking a 5-year NSC at 7.7% **today** guarantees that rate for 5 years regardless of future rate changes.
- If rates fall in Years 2–3, your NSC continues earning 7.7% — you benefit from the lock-in.
- If rates rise significantly, NSC gives no flexibility (it cannot be broken). SBI FD can be broken and reinvested (at the cost of a small penalty).

!!! note "Side heading: Hedge your bets"
    Splitting between NSC (locked rate, max growth) and SBI FD (flexible rate, can reinvest if rates rise) is the classic hedge. See [Chapter 16](16-recommended-plan.md) for the recommended split.
